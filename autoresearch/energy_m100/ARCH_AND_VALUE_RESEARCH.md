### [1] (high/3-0)
Feedback loops in hierarchical/layered (Sugiyama) diagrams are drawn without tangling by treating the graph as a DAG and reversing 'back' edges during layout, then reducing crossings in a separate pass. This is the operational principle behind clean loop routing in dot/dagre/mermaid/TikZ. Actionable rule: lay the main pipeline left-to-right (or top-down), and route the single feedback edge as a reversed back-edge on the outer track so it does not cross forward edges.

Corroborated by Wikipedia (Layered graph drawing) and arXiv:1608.07809 ('Cycle removal: Eliminate all cycles by reversing a preferably small subset of the graph's edges'). ### [5] (high/3-0)
FunSearch justifies its value NOT by a new learning algorithm but by pairing an off-the-shelf pretrained LLM with an automated evaluator inside an evolutionary loop — i.e., contribution framed as a framework/methodology applied to open problems. This is a direct top-venue precedent for 'framework-application, not algorithm-invention' value claims.
**证据**:Nature abstract (verbatim): 'an evolutionary procedure based on pairing a pretrained LLM with a systematic evaluator.' DeepMind blog: 'FunSearch works by pairing a pre-trained LLM... with an automated evaluator.' LLM is frozen/off-the-shelf; no new training algorithm introduced. Nuance: FunSearch does introduce a novel search procedure (islands/best-shot prompting), so 'no new algorithm at all' would overstate — the claim is correctly scoped to 'no new LEARNING algorithm.'
**源**:https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-06924-6

### [6] (high/3-0)
Interpretability is a legitimate value axis independent of raw performance: FunSearch searches over programs (how to solve) rather than solutions (what the answer is), producing compact human-inspectable code that teaches researchers something new. ### [10] (high/3-0)
When a search method does NOT beat a random reference, the accepted framing is to use relative improvement over a randomly-sampled average architecture as the metric, specifically to isolate the search algorithm's own contribution from confounds like engineered search spaces and training protocols. Empirically, across 8 NAS methods on 5 datasets, most fail to significantly beat this random baseline.
**证据**:Yang, Esperança & Carlucci, 'NAS evaluation is frustratingly hard,' ICLR 2020 (arXiv:1912.12522). Finding (verbatim): 'many NAS techniques struggle to significantly beat the average architecture baseline.' Proposed metric: relative improvement over the randomly sampled average architecture, which 'effectively removes advantages arising from expertly engineered search spaces or training protocols.' Corroborated by Sciuto et al. 2019 ('random policy outperforms SOTA NAS') and Li & Talwalkar 2019.
**源**:https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.12522
