# 引导搜索 vs 随机:优势随搜索空间复杂度增长 — deep-research(2026-07)

## 结论(证实用户猜想)
Yes — the literature consistently supports the regularity that guided search (evolutionary / Bayesian / LLM-proposal) only ties random search when the search space is small, low-effective-dimensional, or heavily hand-constrained, and that its advantage grows as the space becomes larger, higher-dimensional, or combinatorially structured. Bergstra & Bengio (2012) show random search matches/beats grid search in a nominally 32-D HPO space precisely because effective dimensionality is low (few hyperparameters matter); they position random search as a BASELINE for adaptive methods, not the ceiling. Wang et al. (2013, REMBO) give the cleanest boundary: standard Bayesian optimization degrades to merely tying random search above a ~15–20 dimension tipping point, and only structure-exploiting methods survive. The NAS "random search is a strong baseline" result (Li & Talwalkar 2019; Sciuto/Yu et al. 2019/2020; Yang et al. 2019) is EXPLICITLY attributed by those authors to over-constrained, expertly-engineered cell-block spaces where any random architecture is good — and when the confounder (weight sharing) is removed, guided samplers beat random by a large margin (P(surpass random) rises 0.07→0.90 for ENAS). At the opposite extreme, FunSearch and AlphaEvolve operate in program/algorithm spaces so vast (cap-set possibilities exceed the atoms in the universe) that brute-force/undirected search is infeasible, and guided search delivers results — including beating Strassen's 4x4 matrix-multiplication algorithm for the first time in 56 years. This directly supports the thesis: the current energy-model task ties random because it is a tiny ~dozen-linear-term space; a larger space (nonlinear combinations, program-level featurization, interaction terms, dozens-to-hundreds of candidates) is exactly the regime where the guided loop is expected to pull ahead.
